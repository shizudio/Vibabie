Generator page · redesign rationale · 20 Sep 2026

What changed, and why it changes the numbers.

Every before on this page describes solmi.ai/ai-music-video-generator as it is live today. Every after is in the prototype sitting next to this file. The page was not restyled — the order in which it asks for things was rebuilt, and the styling followed.

The old page asks a visitor to write a music-video prompt and attach a song before it shows them a single thing they could pick.

Everything needed to solve that was already built — four sample songs, a library, paste-a-link, upload, Create Song. All of it sat behind one button labelled ATTACH A SONG · REQUIRED. The product had already solved the cold start and then hidden the solution behind the word required. The redesign mostly consists of opening that door.

01

The cold start

This page gets the most SEO traffic of any page on the site, which means most of its visitors arrive knowing nothing and owning nothing. The old page opened with two empty required fields. That is the single biggest thing we changed.

The song sources came out from behind the modal

Highest impact

Before

One button: Attach a song · REQUIRED. Behind it, a modal holding paste-a-link, upload, four sample tracks, Create Song and My Songs. A first-time visitor sees none of it, and the word required reads as a wall.

After

A track shelf on the page. Four real songs you can click, and a square + as the first tile holding upload, paste-a-link and Create Song — the same place ChatGPT and Claude put attachment.

Why

A sample the visitor can hear beats an empty required field every time. Nothing was invented here — the same four tracks, the same three sources. They were moved from inside a modal onto the surface, which costs the product nothing and removes the only hard stop on the page.

The prompt box has something in it before you type

Before

An empty textarea with placeholder text. The visitor has to invent the concept, the visual language and the vocabulary the model responds to, from nothing.

After

A row of TRY chips above the box. One click writes a full, working prompt into the field, which the visitor can then edit rather than author.

Why

Editing is a far lower bar than authoring, and the chips double as documentation: they teach what a good Solmi prompt looks like without a tooltip or a help page. They sit above the box, not below, because they are read before the field is used, not after.

02

The page stopped moving

Three separate interactions used to reflow the layout under the cursor. Each one has the same fix: change appearance, not geometry.

Settings expand sideways, not downward

Before

Opening more options pushed a new row into the stack. Everything below it — including the button that spends credits — jumped down, then jumped back on close.

After

More options expands rightwards into the row it already occupies, over 340ms. The settings and the main CTA share one line. Nothing below moves.

Why

A control that moves the primary button is a control that causes mis-clicks. Expanding along the axis with free space keeps the composer a fixed object — and it is what let the settings and the CTA end up on the same row, with 73px of clearance.

Picking a track highlights it instead of collapsing the shelf

Before

Selecting a track collapsed the column and hid every other option. The alternatives vanished at the exact moment the visitor was still deciding between them.

After

The chosen tile lights up and the rest dim. Everything stays where it was, so changing your mind is one click, not a re-open.

Why

Selection is not confirmation. Hiding the alternatives treats a first click as a final answer, and it makes the interface feel like it is shutting doors behind you.

The rail is the same whether you are signed in or not

Before

Thirteen tools listed with labels, permanently. Signing in changed the rail width, so the page reflowed at the moment the visitor started working.

After

Collapsed to icons by default, in both states. A toggle expands it, and that choice is remembered.

Why

This page has one job. Twelve other tool names next to the composer are twelve invitations to leave before making anything — and having an account is not a reason to need more of that list in view.

03

Signal over decoration

A production tool is judged on how fast it can be read, not on how much it explains. Three passes of subtraction.

Confirmation strings removed

Before

Picking a track printed "The Sound of Music selected" underneath it. The same pattern repeated for other controls.

After

Gone. The tile is visibly selected — the state is the confirmation.

Why

Narrating a result the user can already see costs a line of vertical space and adds nothing. Text should appear when something is not visible — a cost, a constraint, an error.

Chevrons removed from the setting chips

Before

Every setting chip carried a ▾, which promises a dropdown. There is no dropdown — the chips cycle their value in place.

After

Plain chips. 18px recovered per chip, which is precisely what made the one-row control bar fit.

Why

An affordance that lies is worse than no affordance: it buys a click that returns nothing the visitor expected. Removing them was correct on its own terms — the space was a bonus.

Every control states its cost, next to the button

Before

Resolution, length and ratio changed what a render costs, and the page did not say so until later in the flow.

After

A live line under the CTA restating render time and credit cost for the exact settings currently picked.

Why

Credits are the unit of trust in a generative product. Showing the price at the moment of the decision — rather than on a pricing page — is what stops the first render from feeling like a gamble.

04

The returning user gets a different page

The old page was identical for a stranger and for someone on their fortieth render. Those are different problems. Toggle Sign in in the prototype to see the second one.

Renders only exist once you have some

Before

A job panel on the page regardless of session — showing a stranger's renders to a first-time visitor, or an empty box.

After

Hidden when signed out. Signed in, it shows what is rendering, what is queued and what is done, with live stage and progress.

Why

State is the thing a landing page cannot have, and it is the reason to come back to a product page rather than to the marketing site. Shown to the wrong person it is noise; shown to the right one it is the whole value.

A render in flight can be edited, not just cancelled

New

Before

A running job offered Cancel and nothing else. The only way to change a video you could already tell was wrong was to throw it away and start over.

After

Edit sits above Cancel, at full contrast. Cancel stays muted underneath.

Why

The storyboard stage is the last point where a change is cheap. After it, the user is paying for a render they already know is wrong. Cancel-or-wait was the wrong pair of choices, and Cancel being the only button made it look like the intended one.

The track shelf becomes your library

Before

My Songs was another tab inside the attach modal — the same two clicks away on every visit, forever.

After

The four samples are replaced by your own tracks in the same shelf. The demo content is scaffolding that removes itself.

Why

Sample content should occupy the slot the real content will later fill, so the layout a visitor learns on day one is the layout they keep. It also means the shelf never has to render an empty state.

05

One design language, two dialects

The generator page and the marketing site did not look like the same company. They do now — without the product page becoming a second landing page.

The page now uses the tokens the site actually ships

Before

A leftover component-library palette, including an orange --primary that zero of the page's 883 elements referenced. The ground was a different black from the rest of the site.

After

The shipped dark tokens: #141614 ground, DM Sans and DM Mono, #1ED760 as the only saturated colour, and the landing page's sweep on the CTA.

Why

Dead tokens are how a codebase drifts: nobody deletes them, so the next page inherits them. Everything on the new page reads from the variables, which is what makes the design system in this folder enforceable rather than advisory.

The tool owns the fold

Before

A marketing headline, a promise and proof above the tool — a second pitch, delivered to people who had already accepted the first one.

After

One mono context line, then the composer, 28px below the top bar. Recipes, FAQ and the rest sit below the fold, for the visitors who want them.

Why

Someone on this page either searched for a music-video generator or came through the pitch already. Re-pitching them costs a screen of scroll and implies the tool is not ready. Depth belongs behind panels, not below the fold.

06

What we deliberately did not change

Redesigns lose things quietly. These were checked and kept.

✓

Every capability in the old attach modal. Upload, paste-a-link, Create Song, My Songs and the four samples all survive — relocated, not removed.

✓

The URL and the page's SEO shape. It remains one indexable page with the FAQ in the markup, which is what the traffic is arriving for.

✓

Every setting. Ratio, resolution, length, style, captions and the rest are all still reachable — the common ones on the row, the rest one expand away.

✓

The brand's one moment of motion. The CTA sweep is the landing page's, unchanged, so the two surfaces share a signature.

✓

Contrast. Body text clears 4.5:1 throughout; the muted token is a solid value rather than an alpha, so it does not drift over different surfaces.

07

Open items for your team

Decisions that need your side of the product, not ours.

Before this ships

  • The Edit route. The Edit button points at /studio/edit/<job>, a placeholder. Point it at the real editor.
  • The four sample tracks. They are the ones from the live modal. Now that they are on the page rather than behind it, they are the first thing a visitor hears — worth choosing on merit.
  • Credit and render-time figures are plausible placeholders. Swap in the real numbers; the line updates from one lookup table.
  • Sign-in is a front-end toggle in the prototype, standing in for your real session state.
